Home Study · Lesson Plan

30 lessons to a scan that runs on its own.

The cross-check is a perishable skill, and it's hard to get enough reps in the airplane. This plan gives you something specific to work on every time you pick up the iPad: a goal, a drill, a standard to hit, and the ACS elements it supports. Work through it in order. Don't move on until you hit the standard.

How to Use It

Short sessions. Honest standards.

Fifteen focused minutes beats an hour of free flying. Every lesson is built to be flown more than once.

01One lesson per session

Pick one, fly it until you hit the standard, then stop. Leave something for next time.

02Talk out loud

Call out what you see and what you're correcting. It slows your scan down to a speed you can fix.

03Hit the standard twice

Once can be luck. Hit it twice in a row, then check the lesson off and move on.

04Bring it to your instructor

Show your CFI or CFII what you worked on. Ground reps make airplane time count for more.

Phase 1 · Lessons 1–5 · Free 6-Pack

6-Pack Foundations

Build the scan on round gauges first. Everything later in the plan sits on top of these five lessons, so don't rush them.

01 The Panel and the Scan6-Pack · 10 min

Goal. Know what every instrument tells you, which ones are primary for each maneuver, and build a scan that always comes back to the attitude indicator.

Set up

6-Pack. Sit the way you'd sit in the airplane, iPad held level in front of you like a yoke. No maneuvering yet.

The drill

  1. Name each of the six instruments out loud and say what it measures: pitch, bank, airspeed, altitude, rate of climb, heading, rate of turn.
  2. Tilt the iPad gently left and right. Watch the attitude indicator, then the turn coordinator, then the heading indicator respond. Say which one moved first.
  3. Practice the radial scan: attitude → airspeed → attitude → altimeter → attitude → heading → attitude. Ten full cycles, out loud.
  4. Now say the primary instrument for straight and level: pitch = altimeter, bank = heading indicator, power = airspeed.

Standard to hit

You can run ten clean scan cycles without your eyes stalling on one gauge, and name primary/supporting for straight and level without hesitation.

Watch for

  • Fixation: staring at one instrument while the others drift.
  • Omission: never looking at the turn coordinator or VSI.
  • Skipping the return to the attitude indicator between gauges.

02 Straight and Level, Hands Light6-Pack · 10–15 min

Goal. Hold altitude and heading with small, early corrections instead of big, late ones.

Set up

6-Pack. Bring the throttle up to a normal cruise power setting and let airspeed settle.

The drill

  1. Pick the current altitude and heading. Fly them for 3 minutes without a single deliberate maneuver.
  2. Every time the altimeter needle moves 20 ft, make a tiny pitch correction on the iPad. Don't wait for 100.
  3. Every time heading moves 2–3°, use a shallow bank (half the deflection you think you need) to come back.
  4. Repeat for a second 3-minute block at a different power setting and note how the pitch for level flight changes.

Standard to hit

Altitude ±100 ft, heading ±10°, airspeed ±10 kt for a full 3-minute block. Then tighten it: aim for half those numbers.

Watch for

  • Chasing the VSI: correct off the altimeter trend, confirm with the VSI.
  • Over-banking a heading correction.
  • Forgetting airspeed changes when you change pitch.

03 Standard-Rate Turns to a Heading6-Pack · 15 min

Goal. Roll into a standard-rate turn, hold it, and roll out on the heading you meant to.

Set up

6-Pack, stabilized straight and level.

The drill

  1. Turn left 90°. Set bank with the attitude indicator, then confirm standard rate on the turn coordinator.
  2. Hold altitude through the turn: expect to add a little back pressure (pitch up slightly) as you roll in.
  3. Lead the rollout by about half your bank angle in degrees (roughly 7–8° for a typical standard-rate turn).
  4. Fly the turn pattern: 90° left, 90° right, 180° left, 180° right, 360° left. Pause level between each one.

Standard to hit

Roll out within ±10° of the target heading, altitude ±100 ft through the turn, bank steady at standard rate.

Watch for

  • Losing altitude in the first 10 seconds of the turn.
  • Rolling out late and overshooting.
  • Letting bank creep steeper as the turn goes on.

04 Climbs, Descents, and Level-Offs6-Pack · 15 min

Goal. Change altitude at a constant airspeed and level off exactly where you meant to.

Set up

6-Pack, stabilized straight and level.

The drill

  1. Climb 1,000 ft: add power, set the climb attitude, and let airspeed settle. Use the throttle lever and pitch together.
  2. Begin the level-off about 10% of your vertical speed early (500 fpm → start at 50 ft before the target).
  3. Descend 1,000 ft: reduce power, set the descent attitude, and hold airspeed constant.
  4. Repeat with a heading change during the climb and during the descent.
  5. Push past the second pitch line on purpose and watch the airspeed bleed and the climb roll over. Recover with pitch down and power, then never fly there again.

Standard to hit

Level off within ±100 ft of the target, airspeed ±10 kt in the climb and descent, heading ±10°.

Watch for

  • Pitching for the climb before adding power.
  • Leveling off late and ballooning through the altitude.
  • Over-pitching: in ChairFly, too much pitch-up bleeds airspeed and the climb turns into a sink.

05 Mixed Cockpit: Changing the Picture6-Pack · Mixed Cockpit · 15 min

Goal. Move your scan between round gauges and a compact glass attitude display without losing control.

Set up

6-Pack with the Mixed Cockpit option turned on.

The drill

  1. Fly straight and level for 2 minutes. Note where your eyes go first now that attitude is on a glass display.
  2. Repeat the Lesson 3 turn pattern. Use the glass attitude display for bank, the round gauges for the rest.
  3. Repeat the Lesson 4 climbs and descents.
  4. Switch Mixed Cockpit off and fly the turn pattern again. Notice what your scan does when the picture changes back.

Standard to hit

Lesson 2–4 standards, held on both panel layouts.

Watch for

  • Staring at the bigger, brighter glass display and dropping the rest of the scan.
  • Losing the heading indicator when it isn't next to the attitude display any more.

Phase 2 · Lessons 6–11 · Glass Cockpit

Glass Cockpit Fundamentals

Same scan, new presentation. Learn to read tapes and trend vectors, use the altitude selector, and fly to an ATC instruction.

06 Reading the Glass PanelGlass Cockpit · 10 min

Goal. Translate everything you learned on the 6-Pack into the glass primary flight display.

Set up

Glass Cockpit, free flight.

The drill

  1. Find each 6-Pack instrument on the glass panel: airspeed tape, attitude, altitude tape, vertical speed, heading, turn rate.
  2. Make a small pitch change. Watch the altitude tape's trend and the vertical speed. Say how far the altitude will move in the next few seconds.
  3. Make a small bank change. Watch the heading and turn rate respond.
  4. Run the same radial scan from Lesson 1, out loud, on the glass panel.

Standard to hit

You can predict where the tapes are heading before they get there, and you can find every value without hunting.

Watch for

  • Reading tape numbers instead of watching their movement.
  • Letting the attitude display take over your whole scan.

07 Straight and Level on TapesGlass Cockpit · 10–15 min

Goal. Hold altitude, heading, and airspeed on the glass panel as tightly as you did on the 6-Pack.

Set up

Glass Cockpit, free flight at cruise power.

The drill

  1. Fly two 3-minute blocks of straight and level, same as Lesson 2.
  2. Correct off the trend: if the altitude tape starts moving, fix the pitch before the number changes much.
  3. Make three power changes (cruise → slower → cruise) and hold altitude through each one.

Standard to hit

Altitude ±100 ft, heading ±10°, airspeed ±10 kt through every power change.

Watch for

  • Big corrections to small deviations.
  • Forgetting to re-trim your hands (relax your grip) after each power change.

08 Turns and Heading DisciplineGlass Cockpit · 15 min

Goal. Turn to an exact heading on the glass panel and roll out on it every time.

Set up

Glass Cockpit, free flight.

The drill

  1. Fly the Lesson 3 turn pattern on the glass panel.
  2. Add timed turns: a standard-rate turn is 3° per second, so a 90° turn should take about 30 seconds. Check yourself against a clock.
  3. Fly a 360° turn and roll out on the original heading without looking away from the panel.

Standard to hit

Roll out within ±10° of the target heading, altitude ±100 ft, bank steady.

Watch for

  • Rolling out on the number instead of leading it.
  • Altitude creeping during long turns.

09 The Altitude Selector and Level-OffsGlass Cockpit · 15 min

Goal. Set your target altitude first, then fly to it and level off on it.

Set up

Glass Cockpit, free flight.

The drill

  1. Before every altitude change, set the new altitude in the altitude selector. Make this a habit, not a step.
  2. Climb 1,000 ft, then 500 ft, then descend 1,500 ft. Lead each level-off by about 10% of vertical speed.
  3. Combine: climb 1,000 ft while turning 90°. Level off and roll out at the same time.

Standard to hit

Every level-off within ±100 ft of the selected altitude, airspeed ±10 kt during the change.

Watch for

  • Changing altitude before setting the selector.
  • Overshooting the level-off because the climb was too aggressive.

10 Pitch + Power = PerformanceGlass Cockpit · 15–20 min

Goal. Build your own table of pitch and power settings so every change starts close to correct.

Set up

Glass Cockpit, free flight. Grab a notepad.

The drill

  1. For each of these, find the throttle position and pitch that hold it, then write them down: cruise level, slow level, cruise climb, cruise descent, approach descent.
  2. Fly each one from memory: set the pitch and power from your table first, then fine-tune.
  3. Fly a sequence: cruise → slow → approach descent → level → climb, using only your table to start each segment.

Standard to hit

Each segment within ±10 kt and on target vertical speed within 30 seconds of setting it.

Watch for

  • Fine-tuning before setting the known numbers.
  • Using pitch alone to fix airspeed on a descent.

11 Flying an ATC ClearanceGlass Cockpit · 10–15 min

Goal. Hear an instruction, understand it, and comply promptly and accurately.

Set up

Glass Cockpit, any scenario with ATC instructions (start with the ILS).

The drill

  1. Before you move anything, read the instruction back out loud like you would on frequency.
  2. Set the selector and heading, then fly it. Time how long it takes you from hearing it to being established.
  3. Fly the first three minutes of the scenario three times. Each time, cut your reaction time.

Standard to hit

Correct readback every time, and a prompt turn and climb or descent toward the assigned heading and altitude.

Watch for

  • Turning the wrong direction for a heading.
  • Starting the climb before setting the new altitude.

Phase 3 · Lessons 12–18 · Glass Cockpit · VOR Hold

Holding

Know where you are, check your entry, and fly the hold to checkride standards until it's boring.

12 Where Am I? Orientation to the StationGlass Cockpit · VOR Hold · 15 min

Goal. Picture your position relative to the station and the hold before you get there.

Set up

VOR Hold scenario.

The drill

  1. When the scenario starts, stop. Say out loud: which side of the station you're on, which way you're headed, and where the hold is.
  2. Sketch the hold on paper: fix, inbound course, turn direction, and an arrow for your current position.
  3. Fly toward the fix and confirm your picture against the map as you get closer.
  4. Restart and repeat until your sketch is right every time before you move the iPad.

Standard to hit

Correct picture of your position and the hold, every time, before you fly.

Watch for

  • Confusing the inbound course with your current heading.
  • Forgetting which way the turns go (standard is right turns).

13 Hold Entries, Part 1: DirectGlass Cockpit · VOR Hold · 15 min

Goal. Figure out your entry before you reach the fix, then check it.

Set up

VOR Hold scenario.

The drill

  1. Read the hold instruction and sketch it, like Lesson 12.
  2. Draw the entry sectors: direct, teardrop, parallel. Decide which sector you're in.
  3. Use the hold-entry popup to check your entry. If you were wrong, work out why before flying.
  4. Fly a direct entry: cross the fix and turn to the outbound heading.

Standard to hit

Correct entry, figured out on your own before you check it, and a prompt turn at the fix.

Watch for

  • Using the popup to find the answer instead of to check it.
  • Turning the wrong way at the fix.

14 Hold Entries, Part 2: TeardropGlass Cockpit · VOR Hold · 15 min

Goal. Fly a teardrop entry and get established on the inbound course without overshooting.

Set up

VOR Hold scenario. Restart until you get a start position in the teardrop sector, or work out the entry for each start you get.

The drill

  1. Check your entry before the fix.
  2. At the fix, turn to the teardrop heading (about 30° off the outbound course, toward the holding side).
  3. Fly outbound about one minute, then turn in the holding direction to intercept the inbound course.

Standard to hit

Correct entry, established inbound with the CDI within ¾ scale, altitude ±100 ft.

Watch for

  • Turning the wrong way back to the inbound course.
  • Rolling out with a big intercept angle and blowing through the course.

15 Hold Entries, Part 3: ParallelGlass Cockpit · VOR Hold · 15 min

Goal. Fly the parallel entry, the one most students mix up.

Set up

VOR Hold scenario, working from parallel-sector starts.

The drill

  1. Check your entry before the fix.
  2. At the fix, turn to parallel the inbound course, flying outbound on the non-holding side.
  3. After about one minute, turn toward the holding side (more than 180°) to return to the fix or intercept inbound.
  4. Say out loud which way you're turning before you turn. This is where most mistakes happen.

Standard to hit

Correct entry and correct turn direction every time, then established inbound within ¾ scale.

Watch for

  • Turning toward the non-holding side on the turn back.
  • Flying outbound on the wrong side of the course.

16 Flying the RacetrackGlass Cockpit · VOR Hold · 15–20 min

Goal. Once you're in the hold, fly it smoothly lap after lap.

Set up

VOR Hold scenario.

The drill

  1. Get established and fly three full laps of the hold.
  2. Time your legs with a watch: aim for a one-minute inbound leg. Adjust the outbound leg to fix the inbound timing.
  3. At the fix each lap, run the 5 Ts out loud: Turn, Time, Twist, Throttle, Talk.

Standard to hit

Airspeed ±10 kt, altitude ±100 ft, heading ±10°, CDI within ¾ scale on the inbound leg.

Watch for

  • Forgetting to start the timer.
  • Altitude drifting in the turns at each end.

17 Holds on Rapid-FireGlass Cockpit · VOR Hold · 15 min

Goal. Make entries automatic by running a lot of them in a short session.

Set up

VOR Hold scenario.

The drill

  1. Restart the scenario ten times. Each time, sketch the hold, figure out your entry, check it, and fly only through the entry turn.
  2. Keep a tally: right on the first try vs. needed the popup.
  3. Any entry you missed, fly it all the way to established inbound.

Standard to hit

9 out of 10 entries right before checking, in under 20 seconds each.

Watch for

  • Guessing to save time. Speed comes from reps, not shortcuts.

18 Holding to Checkride StandardGlass Cockpit · VOR Hold · 15 min

Goal. Fly a full hold, from clearance to two laps, as if an examiner were sitting next to you.

Set up

VOR Hold scenario. Have someone watch, or record your screen.

The drill

  1. Read back the clearance, sketch the hold, figure out the entry, and check it.
  2. Fly the entry and two full laps with no restarts.
  3. Debrief: where did you go outside standards, and why?

Standard to hit

Every Lesson 13–16 standard, held for the whole profile with no restarts.

Watch for

  • Relaxing on lap two. That's where altitude goes.

Phase 4 · Lessons 19–27 · Glass Cockpit · VOR, ILS, LNAV, LNAV+VNAV, Missed

Approaches & the Missed

Every approach type ChairFly offers, from the first intercept to the decision to land or go missed.

19 VOR Approach: Course and StepdownsGlass Cockpit · VOR Approach · 15–20 min

Goal. Fly a non-precision approach: track the course and step down to each published altitude.

Set up

VOR Approach scenario.

The drill

  1. Before you start down, say the final approach course, each stepdown altitude, and the MDA out loud.
  2. Track the course with small heading corrections. Bracket: if you correct 10° and it holds, try 5° back.
  3. At each fix, descend promptly to the next altitude and level off on it.

Standard to hit

CDI within ¾ scale, airspeed ±10 kt, each stepdown altitude held, never below a published altitude.

Watch for

  • Descending before the fix.
  • Chasing the needle with big heading changes.

IR.VI.A.S9IR.VI.A.S11IR.VI.A.S12

20 VOR Approach: Level at the MDAGlass Cockpit · VOR Approach · 15 min

Goal. Get down to the MDA, level off, and hold it until the point where you'd go missed.

Set up

VOR Approach scenario.

The drill

  1. Fly the approach from Lesson 19 down to the MDA.
  2. Level off at the MDA: lead it a little, add power, and hold altitude while you keep tracking the course.
  3. Hold the MDA on course until the end of the approach. Say out loud what you'd do if you don't see the runway.

Standard to hit

MDA +100/−0 ft, CDI within ¾ scale, airspeed ±10 kt.

Watch for

  • Sinking below the MDA during the level-off.
  • Forgetting power at the level-off and losing airspeed.

21 ILS: Intercepting the LocalizerGlass Cockpit · ILS · 15 min

Goal. Turn onto the localizer on time and get established without overshooting.

Set up

ILS scenario.

The drill

  1. Fly the ATC vectors. As the localizer needle comes alive, start your turn to the final course.
  2. Bracket the localizer with small corrections. It gets more sensitive the closer you get.
  3. Fly only to established on the localizer, then restart. Do it five times.

Standard to hit

Established with no overshoot past ¾ scale, airspeed ±10 kt, assigned altitude held until glideslope intercept.

Watch for

  • Turning late and flying through the localizer.
  • Big corrections close in.

22 ILS: Riding the GlideslopeGlass Cockpit · ILS · 15–20 min

Goal. Catch the glideslope and hold it with small pitch and power changes.

Set up

ILS scenario.

The drill

  1. As the glideslope reaches center, reduce power and set your approach descent attitude from your Lesson 10 table.
  2. Glideslope high? Pitch down a hair and pull a little power. Low? The opposite. Small changes only.
  3. Say the needles out loud every few seconds: 'localizer centered, glideslope a dot high, correcting.'

Standard to hit

Localizer and glideslope within ¾ scale, airspeed ±10 kt, a stable descent rate.

Watch for

  • Diving for the glideslope.
  • Fixating on the glideslope and letting the localizer go.

23 ILS: Down to Decision AltitudeGlass Cockpit · ILS · 15 min

Goal. Fly a stable approach all the way to DA and make the call there.

Set up

ILS scenario.

The drill

  1. Set DA in the altitude selector before you start down.
  2. Fly the whole approach, stable, to DA.
  3. At DA, decide out loud: land, or go missed. On this lesson, practice landing it clean.

Standard to hit

Stable all the way to DA: needles within ¾ scale, airspeed ±10 kt, no descent below DA without the runway.

Watch for

  • Getting unstable in the last 500 ft.
  • Hesitating at DA.

24 Going MissedGlass Cockpit · Missed Approach · 15 min

Goal. Turn 'no runway at DA' into a smooth climb out, right away.

Set up

ILS Missed (Missed Approach Practice) scenario.

The drill

  1. Brief the missed approach before you start down: first heading, first altitude, where it ends.
  2. Fly the ILS to DA. At DA: power up, pitch up, say 'going missed.'
  3. Fly the climb out and level off at the missed approach altitude.

Standard to hit

Missed started at DA with no hesitation, altitude ±100 ft, heading ±10°, airspeed ±10 kt on the climb out.

Watch for

  • Pitching up before adding power.
  • Forgetting the first heading because you didn't brief it.

26 RNAV: LNAV ApproachGlass Cockpit · LNAV · 15–20 min

Goal. Fly a GPS approach with lateral guidance only, stepping down to the MDA.

Set up

LNAV approach scenario.

The drill

  1. Brief the approach: waypoints, altitude at each, and the MDA.
  2. Fly waypoint to waypoint with the course needle centered.

Checkride Profiles

Back-to-back profiles flown like a stage check. Grade yourself honestly, or hand the iPad to your instructor.

Goal. Fly a hold and a VOR approach in one sitting, with no restarts.

Set up

VOR Hold, then VOR Approach. Have a CFI watch if you can, or record your screen.

The drill

All Lesson 18 and Lesson 20 standards, no restarts.

For Instructors

“I made ChairFly for students, but it's really starting to feel like ChairFly is made for instructors.”

CFIs and CFIIs are the ones putting ChairFly to work. They use it to brief a hold before anyone straps in, to show an approach as many times as a student needs, and to give students homework that keeps the scan from going stale between lessons. This plan is built to drop straight into your syllabus.

Pull up the scenario for today's lesson and walk the student through it at the table: the entry, the stepdowns, the missed. They show up to the airplane already knowing the picture.

Between flights

Assign a lesson

Give students a lesson number as homework. Each one has a goal and a standard, so you both know what "done" looks like.

After the flight

Fix it on the ground

Student blew a parallel entry? Assign Lesson 15. Sinking below the MDA? Lesson 20. Every lesson lists the ACS elements it supports.

Ground school

Teach it twice

Show holds, intercepts, full approaches, and missed approaches on screen as many times as the class needs, with no airplane time spent.

A simple assign-and-check routine

Step 1Assign

Pick the lesson that matches the next flight, or the thing that went wrong on the last one.

Step 2Practice

Student flies it at home until they hit the standard twice in a row.

Step 3Show you

Before the next flight, the student flies it once in front of you. Five minutes at the table.

Step 4Fly it

Now fly it in the airplane or sim, and see what changed.
